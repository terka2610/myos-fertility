Your eggs, safely stored

Understanding the journey from egg retrieval to centralised cryogenic storage

Your eggs. Your choices. Your future.

If you have chosen to freeze your eggs, you may be wondering what happens to them after they have been collected at your fertility clinic. MYOS is proposing a centralised cryogenic storage service for the long-term storage of frozen eggs (oocytes). This leaflet explains the journey from collection to storage.

1

Your eggs are collected at your fertility clinic

Following ovarian stimulation, your eggs are collected through a procedure called egg retrieval, usually carried out under sedation. The clinic’s embryology team assesses the eggs. Suitable mature eggs are prepared for freezing using a technique called vitrification. This is a rapid freezing method that helps protect eggs by reducing the formation of damaging ice crystals. Once frozen, your eggs are ready to be transferred to their designated storage location.

2

Preparing your eggs for their journey

Your eggs are carefully prepared by the fertility clinic before transportation. They are placed in specially designed, securely identified storage devices suitable for maintaining frozen eggs at extremely low temperatures. Each patient’s eggs are individually identified and documented so their identity and traceability can be maintained throughout the process. The clinic coordinates the transfer with the authorised cryogenic transport provider.

3

Cryogenic transportation: keeping your eggs frozen

Your frozen eggs are transported from the fertility clinic to the MYOS centralised cryogenic storage facility using specialised cryogenic transportation equipment. This equipment is designed to maintain the very low temperatures needed to keep your eggs frozen throughout the journey. The transportation process follows established procedures for handling and transferring frozen reproductive material. Your eggs remain frozen during transportation; they are not thawed and refrozen as part of the transfer process.

4

Arrival at the MYOS centralised cryogenic storage facility

When your eggs arrive, a documented receiving and verification process takes place. Their identification and accompanying records are checked before they are placed in their designated storage location. Your eggs are then stored in specialised cryogenic tanks using liquid nitrogen technology to maintain the extremely low temperatures required for long-term preservation. The facility is designed to provide dedicated storage, monitoring and management of frozen reproductive material.

While your eggs are in storage

Your eggs remain frozen until you decide what you would like to do with them, subject to the applicable consent and storage arrangements. The centralised storage facility is responsible for the ongoing management of the storage environment, including temperature monitoring and relevant safety and contingency procedures. Your eggs remain individually identified and traceable throughout their storage period. You retain the right to make decisions about your stored eggs in accordance with your consent arrangements and applicable regulations.

When you decide to use your eggs

If, in the future, you decide to use your frozen eggs, discuss the next steps with your fertility clinic. The clinic will coordinate the arrangements for the release and transportation of your eggs from the centralised storage facility to the appropriate fertility laboratory. The eggs will then be carefully thawed by the embryology team as part of your fertility treatment. Your fertility specialist will guide you through the available options and explain the process.

The journey at a glance

1 · Fertility clinicEggs are collected, assessed and frozen by vitrification.
2 · PreparationFrozen eggs are securely identified and prepared for transfer.
3 · Cryogenic transportSpecialised equipment maintains the required frozen conditions during the journey.
4 · MYOS storageEggs are received, verified and placed in designated cryogenic storage.

Your questions matter

Will my eggs be thawed during transportation?
No. The purpose of cryogenic transportation is to maintain the required frozen conditions throughout the journey. Your eggs should remain frozen during the transfer.
Will my eggs be mixed with those of other patients?
Your eggs are individually identified and documented. Identification and traceability procedures are followed throughout transportation and storage.
How long can my eggs remain stored?
Storage duration depends on applicable legislation, regulatory requirements and your individual consent arrangements. Your fertility clinic or MYOS can explain the arrangements relevant to you.
Can I decide to use my eggs at a later date?
You can discuss the release of your eggs with your fertility clinic when you are ready, subject to applicable consent and regulatory requirements.
Who do I contact if I have questions?
Your fertility clinic is an important point of contact for questions about your treatment and reproductive choices. MYOS can provide information about centralised storage and transportation arrangements.
Your eggs are important. Their journey matters.

Freezing your eggs is a personal decision and an investment in future possibilities. The proposed MYOS centralised cryogenic storage service aims to support this journey by providing dedicated infrastructure for the preservation and long-term storage of frozen eggs.
